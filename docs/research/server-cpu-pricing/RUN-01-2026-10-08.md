## 1. Experiment objective, bounded population and decision gates

This **first full intake-to-inference research run** creates one reusable evidence slice, not a speculative market-wide discount range. It answers:

1. Are supplier-reported server ASP deltas reproducible and comparable across successive filing vintages?
2. Can dated reference prices for identical CPUs be reconstructed without confusing MSRP/1KU with supplier net ASP?
3. Can OEM public CPU configurator option spreads be normalized across baseline configurations, for Intel and AMD independently?
4. Which proposed negotiated hyperscaler/enterprise or per-SKU margin metrics remain mathematically unidentifiable?

**Scope:** Intel Xeon and AMD EPYC server CPUs; FY2024 comparative disclosure revisions, AMD Oct 2024 9005 launch reference prices versus undated current AMD SKU page, current Intel ARK, two Dell Spain R770 variants and two R7725 variants. No future prices; no full-procurement award panel or performance benchmarks collected in this run. See [methodology](PRICING-OBSERVATORY-METHODOLOGY.md) for larger end-state. The first run's unit of research is a **price observation**, not an end-buyer market forecast.

## 2. Primary-source findings (not supplier invoice prices)

### A. Genuine Intel filing-vintage conflict

The [2024 original Intel 10-K MD&A](https://www.intc.com/filings-reports/all-sec-filings/content/0000050863-25-000009/intc-20241228.htm) reports **FY2024 server ASP +12% YoY** and **server volume −10%** against FY2023. The later [2025 Intel 10-K comparative](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/50863/000005086326000011/intc-20251227.htm) gives **FY2024 ASP +11%** and **server volume −8%** against the same FY2023 period. These are **different reported vintages**, not two independent observations of physical customer-price change. The 2025 report also says FY2025 ASP **−4%** versus FY2024. Keep both FY2024 ASP measurements under distinct source IDs, do not average/cherry-pick, and **do not assert a confirmed cause** without reconciliation to reporting policy, denominator and restatement notes. Source conflict `conflict:run01:intel-fy2024` is open. The earlier pricing packet's `m:pricing:intel-2024-asp` refers to the FY2025 comparative +11% and is not deleted.

Strategic implication: even publicly filed macro ASP percentage series can change with the disclosure vintage or segment boundary, so a naive FY2021–2026 price index has **vintage risk** on top of SKU/buyer-mix risk. Published +12/+11 are **not** evidence of negotiated unit repricing.

### B. Public reference anchors: same SKU, two publication states

[AMD's 10 October 2024 launch table](https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-10-10-amd-launches-5th-gen-amd-epyc-cpus-maintaining-le.html) specifies EPYC 9965 **$14,813 per CPU at 1KU**. The [current mutable product page](https://www.amd.com/es/products/processors/server/epyc/9005-series/amd-epyc-9965.html), as retrieved for this pilot, displays **$11,988 at 1,000 units** for the same CPU. The exact price difference is **−$2,825 (−19.07%)** *between these two reference publications*, **not** necessarily a transaction-price change between October 2024 and October 2026; the repricing effective date is not independently established. No frozen HTML or digest exists yet. Also staged from the same launch table: EPYC 9015 $527, 9255 $2,495, 9355 $3,694, 9555 $9,826; they are cross-SKU launch anchors, **not a representative shipment-weighted average**.

Intel's [Xeon 6980P ARK page](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/products/sku/240777/intel-xeon-6980p-processor-504m-cache-2-00-ghz/specifications.html) displays **$13,955 Recommended Customer Price** for a 128-core part (as retrieved). The page explicitly says RCP is guidance, **not a formal pricing offer**. No verifiable dated historical official ARK price snapshot was ingested for this CPU: the pilot therefore **does not compute an Intel matched-SKU repricing rate**, or equate AMD 192 cores with Intel 128 cores on sticker price alone.

### C. OEM option-price normalization — two independent models

Dell's public Spain configurators display **CPU selection increments relative to different selected server baselines**, not the OEM's cost of buying the CPU. In [R7725 baseline EPYC 9015](https://www.dell.com/es-es/shop/servidores-almacenamiento-y-redes/poweredge-r7725/spd/poweredge-r7725/gl_promo_per7725_1), selecting 9355 is **+€4,589.89**; in [R7725 baseline EPYC 9355](https://www.dell.com/es-es/shop/cty/pdp/spd/poweredge-r7725/emea_r7725), selecting 9015 is **−€4,589.89**. The implied *9355-minus-9015 option premium* is the same regardless of selected baseline.

For Xeon, the [R770 baseline 6520P](https://www.dell.com/es-es/shop/servidores-almacenamiento-y-redes/poweredge-r770/spd/poweredge-r770/promo_r770_1?view=configurations) shows 6515P **−€1,172.25** while [R770 baseline 6515P](https://www.dell.com/es-es/shop/servidores-almacenamiento-y-redes/poweredge-r770/spd/poweredge-r770/promo_r770_2?view=configurations) shows 6520P **+€1,172.25**. Other matched options across these two page pairs yield the same baseline-adjusted price deltas in the supplied snapshot panel. The deterministic evaluator checks all overlapping SKUs with cent-level arithmetic. This is an **internal consistency check of Dell's displayed option ladder**, not identification of AMD/Intel CPU gross-to-net realized pricing.

**Critical scope controls:** source retrieval as-of is 2026-10-08, but the web results may be cached from different days; no synchronized point-in-time Dell carts were replayed. Both pages warn that choosing a different CPU may cause other config changes. PSU/heatsink/second-CPU/licensing/memory changes remain unverified. Do not compare absolute displayed server prices across different Dell promotions. Do not assume displayed amounts include or exclude VAT unless confirmed. One reference 9965 option is unavailable in the low-end R7725 configuration but listed in the other; *availability is censored, not zero.*

The CLI prints only **derived descriptive evidence**: the FY2024 +12/+11 vintage mismatch, AMD published-reference 9965 change, two Dell option-spread equivalence checks, and a list of unidentifiable outputs. It fails on orphan sources, malformed price layers, duplicate SKUs, wrong selected baselines, incompatible quote currencies or >€0.01 delta inconsistency. Tests include deliberate corruption to confirm rejection. **There is no estimated hyperscaler discount output.** A successful pilot test is not validation of procurement price accuracy.

## 4. Decision implications and disconfirming interpretations

**Supplier-side:** Aggregate server ASP can change from portfolio/core-count mix, buyer-class mix or net repricing. A matched-SKU historical reference decline is evidence about public pricing posture, not proof of margin contraction or realized net discounts. Capacity scarcity and Arm outside options determine pricing leverage, but are hypotheses until measured. The 2024 +12/+11 conflict proves financial-vintage consistency must be a model gate.

**Buyer-side:** OEM CPU option premiums can be normalized as a *relative choice cost* within a published Dell configuration, but never subtracted from AMD's 1KU as if the residual were Dell margin. Enterprise bids have full BOM and services; hyperscalers may source through ODM or direct. Need qualified workload throughput, power, licenses, resiliency and software migration to estimate buyer maximum willingness to pay. Cross-model/region comparisons require confirmed equivalent BOM and SLA.

**Alternative interpretations:** AMD's 9965 current 1KU change may reflect catalog merchandising, lifecycle/volume repricing or page revisions; no effective date can be assigned. Two agreeing Dell quote ladders may share the same underlying catalog calculation, thus are **not independent confirmations of a supplier invoice**. Intel's FY2024 filing change may be a methodological reassignment or comparative recast; unresolved until accounting review. Those limitations are decisions, not noise to suppress.
